#### Synthesis: South Yorkshire's Position in Key Industrial Strategy Sectors

The following analysis synthesises findings from the South Yorkshire Investment Playbook across four priority sectors: **Life Sciences**, **Clean Energy**, **Defence**, and **Advanced Manufacturing**. Advanced Manufacturing acts as a cross-cutting enabler connecting the other three sectors.

##### Overview: The Cross-Cutting Role of Advanced Manufacturing

Advanced Manufacturing is not simply one sector among many in South Yorkshire—it is the foundational capability that underpins and connects Life Sciences, Clean Energy, and Defence. The region's manufacturing legacy, combined with applied research capabilities at institutions like the University of Sheffield and the AMRC, creates a "capabilities feedback loop" where research informs local industry and local production strengthens innovation (Slides 2, 10, 13).

Key cross-cutting Advanced Manufacturing capabilities include:
- **Industrial Machinery & Tools**: Ranked #1 among MCAs for jobs, #2 for firms, #3 for UKRI spending (Slide 16). This subsector supports production across all priority sectors.
- **Metal & Chemical Materials Manufacturing**: Ranked #2 for jobs, #3 for firms—essential for defence components, clean energy infrastructure, and medical equipment (Slides 9, 16).
- **Engineering & Technical Consulting**: High R&I ranking (#2 nationally) with 11,250 jobs, providing design and process expertise across sectors (Slides 19, 30).

---

##### Life Sciences

**Current Position:**
- South Yorkshire has approximately **68,000-71,000 jobs** in Life Sciences, representing 12% of total regional employment—the largest share of any priority sector (Slide 8).
- The sector shows **2.1% CAGR** job growth (2019-2024), slightly below the UK average of 3.3%.
- Location Quotient of 1.3 indicates moderate specialisation, ranking #6 among MCAs (Slide 9).

**Strengths:**
- **Healthcare & Medical Activities**: 60,430 jobs with R&I ranking #3 nationally (Slide 16). This subsector shows co-located innovation and production capabilities.
- **Medical & Pharmaceutical Manufacturing Equipment**: Ranked #1 among MCAs for both jobs (2,100) and firms, with the highest Product Complexity Index (0.03) among South Yorkshire's specialised subsectors (Slides 16, 26).
- The University of Sheffield ranks #3 nationally for Healthcare & Medical Activities R&I funding, capturing £3.4M (Slide 21).

**Advanced Manufacturing Connection:**
- Medical device manufacturing relies heavily on precision engineering, machining, and materials science capabilities present in South Yorkshire's advanced manufacturing base.
- The region shows diversification opportunities into **Medical and Biotechnology R&I** and **Medical and Pharmaceutical Manufacturing Products**—activities that leverage existing advanced manufacturing capabilities (Slides 27, 28).

**Gaps & Opportunities:**
- R&I investment in Life Sciences (£68.1M) shows a low LQ of 0.6, ranking #10—indicating research capacity lags behind the production footprint (Slide 9).
- Medical & Biotechnology R&I shows only 798 jobs but represents a diversification opportunity with moderate complexity (PCI 0.69) (Slides 26, 28).

---

##### Clean Energy

**Current Position:**
- Approximately **11,300-12,500 jobs** in Clean Energy Industries, representing 2.1% of regional employment (Slide 8).
- Sector shows **1.8% CAGR** job growth, matching the UK average.
- Location Quotient of 1.4 indicates above-average specialisation, ranking #8 among MCAs (Slide 9).

**Strengths:**
- **Sustainable Mobility**: Ranked #1 nationally for R&I (£8.9M), ranked #3 for jobs (2,770). This is a flagship co-located innovation and production strength (Slides 16, 17).
- **Clean Power Hydrogen**: Ranked #2 for jobs (460), #3 for R&I (£1.2M)—a small but rapidly growing subsector with significant co-location of capabilities (Slide 16).
- **Waste Management**: Ranked #1 for firms, #4 for jobs (3,435)—strong productive base (Slide 16).
- The University of Sheffield ranks #1 nationally for Sustainable Mobility R&I funding (£8.9M) and #3 for Other Clean Energy Areas (£4.8M) (Slide 21).

**Recent Investments:**
- **Fidra Energy** (Sept 2025): ~£1bn for UK's largest Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) at Thorpe Marsh, Doncaster—capable of supplying ~785,000 homes (Slide 11).
- **Holtec** (May 2025): £1.5bn to build a Small Modular Reactor (SMR) factory (Slide 11).

**Advanced Manufacturing Connection:**
- Clean energy infrastructure (wind turbines, hydrogen electrolysers, battery systems, SMRs) requires advanced materials, precision components, and specialised manufacturing processes.
- South Yorkshire's strengths in **Basic Metals** (ranked #1 for jobs with LQ 9.4) and **Metal & Chemical Materials Manufacturing** directly support clean energy supply chains (Slides 16, 24).
- 37% of UKRI funding to South Yorkshire institutions in Clean Energy involves partners from outside the region, indicating national supply chain integration (Slide 19).

**Gaps & Opportunities:**
- Energy Generation and Energy Transmission & Distribution show low job rankings (#9, #10) but moderate R&I positioning—opportunities to build production around research strengths (Slides 14, 19).
- Nuclear Industry has very few direct jobs but strong firm presence (LQ 3.2) and is positioned for growth with SMR investments (Appendix E).

---

##### Defence

**Current Position:**
- Approximately **14,580-15,900 jobs** in Defence OEMs, representing 2.5% of regional employment (Slide 8).
- Sector shows **-2.1% CAGR** job decline (2019-2024), against UK growth of 0.9%—though this may be reversing with new investments (Slide 8).
- Overall Defence LQ of 0.7 ranks #12 among MCAs, but this masks significant upstream specialisations (Slide 9).

**Strengths:**
- **South Yorkshire plays a special role in the UK defence industrial base** through upstream manufacturing rather than final assembly (Slide 2).
- **Basic Iron & Steel Manufacturing**: LQ of 8.4—South Yorkshire is the UK's premier region for specialist steel production essential for defence applications including gun barrels and nuclear submarines (Slide 24).
- **Metal Structures**: LQ of 1.4 with 1,235 jobs at firms like MTL Advanced Ltd (Slide 24).
- **Manufacture of Fasteners & Screw Machine Products**: LQ of 5.3 at firms like Arnold Wragg Limited (Slide 24).
- **Manufacture of Other Electrical Equipment**: LQ of 2.4 with 1,371 jobs, including firms like Magnetic Systems Technology Ltd (Slide 24).
- The University of Sheffield ranks #2 nationally for Air and Spacecraft Machinery R&I funding (£3.8M), behind only Imperial College London (Slide 21).

**Key Defence Contractors in Region:**
- **BAE Systems** (Sheffield): R&D in artillery development; production of 155mm lightweight howitzer units (Slide 11).
- **Sheffield Forgemasters**: R&D in large steel castings, forgings, welding technologies; heavy steel components for defence, nuclear, aerospace. Capitalisation increased from £900M to £1.3bn (Slides 10, 11).
- **Advanced Manufacturing (Sheffield) Limited**: High-precision metallic parts for aerospace and defence (Slide 11).

**Recent Investments:**
- **New Defence Growth Deals** (Sept 2025): ~£50M fund creating up to 50,000 defence jobs nationally; South Yorkshire identified as key for specialist steel (Slide 11).
- **Sheffield Forgemasters** (June 2025): Capitalisation increase to £1.3bn for defence manufacture expansion (Slide 11).
- **Walsin Lihwa** (July 2025): Upgrade of Special Melted Products factory, creating 200+ jobs by 2028 (Slide 11).

**Advanced Manufacturing Connection:**
- Defence is fundamentally dependent on advanced manufacturing capabilities. 77% of UKRI funding to South Yorkshire institutions in Defence involves partners from outside the region—the highest share of any sector—indicating the region's role as a national manufacturing hub for defence supply chains (Slide 19).
- Air & Spacecraft Machinery shows high R&I ranking (#4) but low job ranking (#9)—a diversification opportunity to translate research into local production (Slides 14, 28).

**Gaps & Opportunities:**
- **Diversification opportunities** identified in: Air & Spacecraft Machinery, IT & Telecommunications Services, Other Defence-related Services (Slides 27, 28, 30).
- South Yorkshire can expand from upstream components into higher-complexity defence activities by leveraging existing capabilities.

### South Yorkshire Growth Plan 2025–2035

**Source:** South Yorkshire Mayoral Combined Authority
**Date:** September 2025
**File:** `sources/45525-SY-Growth-Plan-Final-2025-09-30-2.pdf`

#### Synthesis: South Yorkshire's Position in Key Industrial Strategy Sectors

The Growth Plan articulates South Yorkshire's ten-year vision for economic growth, organised around four missions (Businesses, Places, People, Future) and eight strategic objectives. The document reinforces the region's position in the National Industrial Strategy priority sectors, with Advanced Manufacturing serving as the foundational cross-cutting capability connecting Life Sciences, Clean Energy, and Defence.

##### Advanced Manufacturing: The Cross-Cutting Enabler

The Growth Plan positions Advanced Manufacturing as the region's core competitive advantage and the foundation upon which other priority sectors depend:

**Current Position:**
- Contributes **£3.4bn in GVA** and accounts for **12.5% of the regional economy**—defying national trends with ongoing sector growth
- **Ranks #2 in the UK** for advanced manufacturing innovation cluster size
- Home to globally recognised capabilities built around the University of Sheffield Advanced Manufacturing Research Centre (AMRC), which collaborates with over 120 companies

**Core Capabilities:**
- Advanced materials (metals and composites)
- Precision engineering
- Digital manufacturing and AI-powered production optimisation
- Machining and assembly systems
- Additive and metals manufacturing
- Composites research (the COMPASS project has propelled the region to the forefront of global composites research)

**Key Assets:**
- **AMRC**: A globally recognised network of innovation centres specialising in machining, composites, design, additive manufacturing, and manufacturing intelligence
- **Factory 2050**: The UK's first fully reconfigurable digital factory
- **Royce Translational Centre**: Specialising in advanced materials research and translation into industry
- **Anchor companies**: Boeing (first European production facility), McLaren Automotive, Rolls-Royce

**Cross-Sector Role:**
The Growth Plan explicitly states that the region's competitive advantage lies "at the intersections: where advanced manufacturing meets clean energy, where digital capabilities enhance health and life sciences, and where materials science underpins defence and aerospace." This positions Advanced Manufacturing not as a standalone sector but as the enabling capability that powers the other three priority sectors.

##### Life Sciences

**Current Position:**
- Contributes **£3.3 billion GVA** (12.3% of regional output)
- **Ranks 4th among UK Core Cities** for MedTech innovation cluster size
- Strength lies in translational research partnerships and precision manufacturing of medical devices
- Home to the **largest cluster of medical device manufacturers in the country**

**Core Capabilities:**
- Medical technology and manufacture
- Gene therapy
- Preventative health

**Key Research Assets:**
- **NCCHT** (National Centre for Child Health Technology): World-first integrated healthcare system for children and young people at Olympic Legacy Park
- **AWRC** (Advanced Wellbeing Research Centre): Dedicated to improving health through movement innovations
- **SITraN** (Sheffield Institute for Translational Neuroscience): Researching motor neuron disease and neurodegenerative disorders
- **GTIMC** (Gene Therapy Innovation & Manufacturing Centre)
- **Medical AMRC**: Creating manufacturing-healthcare collaborations for sustainable medical products and smart hospitals
- **NCSEM** (National Centre for Sport & Exercise Medicine)
- **Insigneo Institute**: Research at the interface of healthcare, engineering and science

**Key Businesses:**
- **BBraun**: Multiple medical device company with 63,000 global staff
- **Canon Medical**: Present at Olympic Legacy Park with Digital Health Hub
- **Swann Morton**: Development of surgical instruments
- **Sheffield Precision Medical**: Leader in orthopaedic contract manufacturing
- **Rinri Therapeutics**: Regenerative cell therapy for hearing loss
- **Exciting Instruments**: Single-molecule detection platform for drug development and diagnostics (£4m seed funding secured)
- **STEPS Rehabilitation**: Specialist neurological and trauma rehabilitation

**Connection to Advanced Manufacturing:**
Life sciences growth depends heavily on South Yorkshire's precision manufacturing capabilities. The Medical AMRC explicitly bridges advanced manufacturing and healthcare, applying the latest manufacturing technologies to medical products. The region's strengths in medical device manufacture rely on the same precision engineering, machining, and materials science capabilities that underpin the broader advanced manufacturing cluster.

**Growth Opportunities:**
- Leveraging precision manufacturing for global medical device markets
- Developing digitally driven health solutions
- Serving as NHS innovation testbeds
- Expanding the Olympic Legacy Park ecosystem
- Bridging Medical AMRC research to production
- Capitalising on regional AI capabilities for digital health solutions

##### Clean Energy

**Current Position:**
- Has the **largest share of the UK's CleanTech economy at 6.9%**—more than three times the share of Greater Manchester and the West Midlands
- **UK's fourth largest Net Zero cluster**, contributing **£1.6 billion annually**
- Per capita carbon emissions falling across all four local authorities
- Home to industrial innovation capabilities for manufacturing decarbonised power

**Core Capabilities:**
- Manufacture of hydrogen production and storage equipment
- Nuclear technologies (SMR development and fusion)
- Sustainable aviation fuels
- Carbon capture
- Industrial decarbonisation solutions

**Key Research Assets:**
- **University of Sheffield Energy Institute**: Over 300 global researchers addressing major energy challenges
- **Translational Energy Research Centre (TERC)**: National pilot-scale testing facility offering access to low-carbon energy research
- **Sustainable Aviation Fuels Innovation Centre (SAF-IC)**: Supporting SAF development and testing
- **UK SAF Clearing House**: One of only two in the world, providing support for SAF development, testing, qualification and production
- Together, TERC and SAF-IC form the **UK's first integrated facility** capable of capturing CO₂, producing green hydrogen, converting it into sustainable aviation fuels, and analysing performance in a single location

**Key Businesses:**
- **ITM Power**: Operates the world's largest electrolyser manufacturing site in Sheffield—a global leader in green hydrogen technology
- **Clean Power Hydrogen**: Specialises in membrane-free electrolyser technology
- **Suiso**: Active in hydrogen manufacturing ecosystem
- **EON**: Operates Blackburn Meadows renewable energy plant and district heating network
- **GeoPura, Lhyfe**: Commercial hydrogen producers in Doncaster and Sheffield

**Connection to Advanced Manufacturing:**
The Growth Plan emphasises that clean energy infrastructure (wind turbines, hydrogen electrolysers, battery systems, SMRs, nuclear components) requires advanced materials, precision components, and specialised manufacturing processes. The AMRC supports the clean energy sector through:
- Collaborating with companies like Rolls-Royce to enhance technologies
- Developing clean energy hydrogen technologies
- Helping manufacturers scale up production of competitive offshore wind technologies

The region's strengths in specialist steel production (via Sheffield Forgemasters) and advanced materials directly enable clean energy supply chains.

**Growth Opportunities:**
- Exporting hydrogen systems and industrial decarbonisation solutions to markets experiencing net-zero transitions
- Leading global sustainable aviation fuel development through the SAF-IC and SAF Clearing House
- Leveraging nuclear capabilities for global SMR and fusion markets
- Transforming foundational industries (responsible for 10% of UK CO₂ emissions) through hydrogen and carbon reduction technologies
- Creating resilient regional energy systems
- Establishing market-led hydrogen hubs with guaranteed demand through defence and heavy industry contracts
- Developing Doncaster Sheffield Airport area as a national clean energy testbed

##### Defence

**Current Position:**
- South Yorkshire has emerged as a **critical UK defence research hub** with unique Ministry of Defence ownership of key assets
- Identified as **one of five leading places in the UK** in the defence industrial economy under the recent Defence Growth Deal
- Strategic importance reinforced by significant government investment

**Core Capabilities:**
- Nuclear-grade steel production
- Advanced materials for defence applications
- Energetics research
- Autonomous systems development
- Communications and networks
- Cyber and dual-use technology innovation

**Key Research Assets:**
- **University of Sheffield AMRC**: Supporting defence manufacturing and supply chains
- **Blast and Impact Dynamics Lab**
- **Henry Royce Centre DSTL-funded Defence Materials Centre of Excellence (DMEx)**
- **Laboratory for Validation and Verification (LVV)**
- **Integrated Civil and Infrastructure Research Centre (ICAIR)**

**Key Businesses:**
- **Sheffield Forgemasters** (MOD-owned): Produces ultra-clean nuclear-grade steel for submarine reactors and fusion energy projects, leveraging the Nuclear AMRC's research. Capitalisation increased from £900M to £1.3bn for defence manufacture expansion
- **BAE Systems**: The UK's largest defence contractor, opening a new **£25 million artillery development and manufacturing facility** in Sheffield (creating 50 new jobs)
- **Magtec**: Rotherham-based SME funded by the Defence Technology Exploitation Programme (DTEP) to develop hybrid-electric drive systems for military vehicles
- **SMP**: Manufacturer of premium speciality steels and nickel-based Super Alloys for aerospace, oil & gas, and civil nuclear industries

**Connection to Advanced Manufacturing:**
Defence is fundamentally dependent on advanced manufacturing capabilities. The Growth Plan notes that South Yorkshire plays a "special role in the UK defence industrial base" through upstream manufacturing rather than final assembly. The region provides:
- Specialist steel essential for defence applications including gun barrels and nuclear submarines
- High-precision metallic parts for aerospace and defence
- Advanced materials science feeding into aerospace, nuclear and defence supply chains

The AMRC network provides the translational research and manufacturing capabilities that defence contractors rely upon. The presence of companies like Boeing, Rolls-Royce, and McLaren—all serving both civilian and defence markets—demonstrates the dual-use nature of the region's manufacturing base.

**Growth Opportunities:**
- Attracting defence companies from NATO allies seeking UK partnerships
- Commercialising defence innovations for civilian aerospace and automotive markets
- Integrating regional SMEs into global defence supply chains
- Civilian spin-offs transferring defence technologies to autonomous systems, cybersecurity, and advanced materials
- Applying defence materials science across clean energy, manufacturing, and aerospace
- Developing secure innovation environments enabling civilian commercialisation of dual-use technologies
- Establishing a defence research consortium leveraging MOD relationships
- Connecting regional manufacturing capabilities with defence procurement needs

##### Strategic Implications: Interconnected Sector Development

The Growth Plan reinforces the interconnected nature of South Yorkshire's priority sectors through several mechanisms:

**Cluster Leadership:**
- The plan calls for clear leadership structures for each major cluster
- Emphasis on strengthening supply chain development around anchors
- Promoting collaboration between firms to share knowledge and adopt new technologies
- Ensuring clusters have visible national and international profile

**Connected Innovation Assets:**
- Focus on strengthening networks between digital firms, manufacturers, healthcare providers, and clean energy innovators
- Operating innovation districts on a "hyper-local scale, with large anchors tightly enmeshed with suppliers and research institutions"
- Using public procurement to reward innovation and anchor institutions into supporting local supply chains

**Cross-Sector Talent Pipeline:**
- Co-designing curricula, apprenticeships and pathways with businesses
- Expanding training pathways linked to advanced manufacturing, health and green industries
- Addressing the 'missing middle' of technical skills at Levels 3–5
- AMRC Training Centre trains 1,360+ apprentices annually up to degree level

**Investment Zone:**
- South Yorkshire is home to the UK's first Investment Zone
- Every £1 of public investment returned nearly £9 of private investment in the first year
- The IZ is a critical vehicle for delivering growth across all priority sectors

#### Context and Purpose

This study maps South Yorkshire's health and wellbeing innovation ecosystem, identifying assets, capabilities, partnerships, and gaps. It complements the Investment Playbook and Growth Plan by providing granular detail on the health tech sector, with particular emphasis on digital health capabilities, innovation infrastructure, and opportunities for cross-sector integration.

#### Key Findings: Health Tech Assets Beyond Existing Synthesis

##### Digital Health and Technology Capabilities

While the Growth Plan identifies South Yorkshire as the UK's #4 MedTech innovation cluster, the KADA study reveals **significant additional digital health strengths**:

### South Yorkshire's Economic Position - Methodology Note

**Source:** New Localism Associates
**Date:** October 2025
**File:** `sources/South Yorkshire's Economic Position - Methodology Note – October 2025.pdf`

#### Methodological Summary: Foundation of the Investment Playbook Analysis

This methodology note documents the rigorous analytical framework underpinning the South Yorkshire Investment Playbook, creating what may be the UK's first unified, reproducible platform for assessing regional economic performance across priority industrial sectors. The approach addresses a critical gap: prior to this work, no standardised method existed for comparing regional strengths and gaps across UK priority sectors and subsectors in both absolute and relative terms.

##### Core Methodological Innovation

The framework integrates four distinct data streams into a unified analytical platform:
- **Production capabilities**: Jobs (ONS/BRES 2018-2023), Firms (Companies House 2018-2023)
- **Research & Innovation capabilities**: Public R&I spending (UKRI 2012-2024), Patents (OECD REGPAT 2018-2024)

The innovation lies in harmonising these disparate datasets through (i) consistent sector/subsector taxonomies, (ii) geographic alignment to Mayoral Combined Authorities, and (iii) standardised annual time series. All processing is implemented in R to maximise transparency and reproducibility.

##### Sector Classification Framework

**Priority Sectors**: The analysis adopts the UK government's eight Invest 2035 high-growth sectors plus a ninth sector—Upstream Manufacturing & Logistics—specifically included to capture South Yorkshire's industrial profile. Sectors were prioritised based on median wages above the national average and MIT classification as "tradable" industries.

**Subsector Taxonomy**: Within each sector, the framework defines granular subsectors to capture specific strengths and comparative advantages. For example, Advanced Manufacturing includes Electronic & Technology Equipment Mfg., Industrial Machinery & Tools, Metal & Chemical Materials Mfg., and Engineering & Technical Consulting. This sector-subsector structure is applied consistently across all four data dimensions.

**Geographic Scope**: Analysis covers 11 MCAs plus Greater London, enabling like-for-like comparisons. MCAs analysed include Cambridgeshire & Peterborough, East Midlands, Greater Manchester, Liverpool City Region, North East, South Yorkshire, Tees Valley, West Midlands, West of England, West Yorkshire, and York and North Yorkshire. University benchmarking additionally includes Oxford.

##### Keyword-Based Classification for R&I and Patents

For UKRI grants and patents—which lack standard industrial classification codes—the team developed a custom keyword-based classification system through "exhaustive bibliographic review." This involved:

1. Creating a curated dictionary mapping sector-subsector pairs to domain-specific terms and synonyms
2. Scanning text fields in UKRI and patent records to flag matches with subsector vocabularies
3. Implementing three refinements to improve accuracy:
   - **Word-boundary detection** for ambiguous short tokens (AI, IT, ship, iron, SAF) to avoid false positives
   - **Targeted disambiguation rules** (e.g., excluding Life Sciences records mentioning "iron" as a chemical element from Basic Metals classification)
   - **Manual validation**: Three rounds of review examining top grants per subsector and unassigned grants to refine the dictionary

Records can match multiple subsectors; those without sufficient textual evidence remain unclassified. The keyword dictionary is publicly accessible for transparency and refinement.

##### Processing Public R&I (UKRI) Data: Addressing Collaboration and Time Allocation

UKRI data required sophisticated processing to enable fair regional comparisons:

**Temporal Allocation**: UKRI grants span multiple years but lack in-year expenditure profiles. The framework applies linear time-splitting, dividing each grant equally across quarterly periods between project start and end dates. This quarterly approach balances precision (avoiding noisy monthly splits) with capturing within-year variation while preserving annual comparability.

**Collaboration Attribution**: UKRI identifies a single lead organisation but collaborative projects involve multiple partners. To reflect distributed R&I activity, the framework adopts a **50/50 split rule**:
- 50% of funding attributed to the lead institution
- Remaining 50% divided equally among all collaborators
- Projects without collaborators allocate 100% to the lead

This hybrid approach recognises the lead's administrative responsibility while crediting material participation by collaborators. Alternative schemes (100% to lead, proportional splits) were tested for sensitivity checks but are not used in the main analysis.

**Institution Normalisation**: Institution names were standardised using curated equivalence tables (e.g., "The University of Sheffield" → "University of Sheffield") and mapped to MCAs based on geographic location.

**Centres**: Large multi-year grants for research hubs ("Centres") are flagged but included in headline figures to acknowledge their scale and strategic relevance.

**Scenario Testing**: Eight allocation scenarios were defined by combining three binary dimensions (collaboration split, centres inclusion, temporal allocation). The 50/50 quarterly split with centres included was selected as the main scenario after review.

##### Key Metrics Calculated

The framework generates several analytical outputs:

**Location Quotient (LQ)**: Measures regional specialisation relative to the UK average. LQ > 1 indicates above-average concentration; higher values signal stronger specialisation.

**Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR)**: Measures job growth trends 2019-2024 to identify expanding or contracting sectors.

**MCA Rankings**: Ranks South Yorkshire against other MCAs across jobs, firms, R&I spending, and patents for each subsector.

**Product Complexity Index (PCI)**: Sourced from economic complexity literature to identify high-value diversification opportunities (referenced in Appendix D).

**Per-capita indicators**: All metrics normalised using ONS mid-year population estimates to enable size-adjusted comparisons.

##### Framework for Categorising Subsectors

The Playbook organises subsectors into three strategic categories based on their MCA rankings across production and innovation metrics:

1. **Co-located Production & Innovation**: Subsectors ranking highly in BOTH jobs and R&I spending (e.g., Sustainable Mobility ranked #3 for jobs, #1 for R&I)
2. **Specialised Niches**:
   - **Production strengths**: High job/firm rankings but lower R&I (e.g., Waste Management)
   - **Innovation strengths**: High R&I rankings but lower jobs (e.g., Engineering & Technical Consulting)
3. **Diversification Opportunities**: Subsectors with high Product Complexity Index and density of related capabilities, indicating feasible pathways to higher value-added activities

This categorisation informs targeted investment strategies: co-located strengths warrant cluster development, production niches may attract R&I, innovation niches may catalyse firm formation, and diversification opportunities guide capability-building.

| Variable | Source | Coverage | Granularity |
|----------|--------|----------|-------------|
| Jobs | ONS/BRES | 2018-2023 | SIC 2007 5-digit subclass |
| Firms | Companies House/ONS | 2018-2023 | SIC 2007 5-digit subclass |
| Public R&I | UKRI Gateway to Research | 2012-2024 | Project-level with collaborator API data |
| Patents | OECD REGPAT | 2018-2024 | Patent records |

Additional sources reviewed but not integrated: HESA (institutional research income), Dimensions (Manufacturing Engineering subset for validation).

##### Acknowledged Limitations

The methodology note transparently documents several constraints:

**Sector/subsector mapping**: Accuracy depends on source descriptor fidelity (SIC codes for jobs/firms; keywords for R&I/patents). Some grants lack detailed descriptions and remain unclassified. Firms engaging in multiple activities may be registered under only one SIC code.

**Geographic alignment**: Reported locations may reflect headquarters rather than operational sites, potentially blurring MCA comparisons.

**Time coherence**: Different data vintages and release calendars introduce publication lags and revision effects despite annual harmonisation.

**UKRI-specific limitations**:
- Coverage limited to public R&I awards (excludes private R&D)
- Temporal allocation uses linear distribution; actual spend profiles may vary
- 50/50 collaboration split is an analytical choice; alternative splits yield different territorial allocations
- Keywords may evolve over time, affecting classification consistency

**Jobs/Firms limitations**: Companies may not be registered under appropriate SIC codes; official nomenclature may not accurately reflect operations.

**Patents limitations**: Publication/application lags delay signals; keyword assignment may under-capture interdisciplinary filings.

##### How This Differs From Standard Approaches

This methodology advances regional economic analysis in several ways:

**Unified data platform**: Integrates production and innovation metrics into a single framework, enabling holistic assessment of regional capabilities rather than examining jobs or R&I in isolation.

**Transparent keyword classification**: Makes subsector assignment logic explicit and reproducible through public dictionaries, unlike proprietary classification systems.

**Collaboration-aware R&I allocation**: The 50/50 split provides more realistic territorial allocation than standard approaches that assign 100% to lead institutions, better reflecting distributed innovation networks.

**Subsector granularity**: Goes beyond broad sector analysis to identify specific capability strengths (e.g., distinguishing Sustainable Mobility from Energy Generation within Clean Energy).

**MCA-level comparability**: Creates like-for-like regional benchmarking across consistent geographies, addressing the absence of standardised MCA comparison frameworks.

**Reproducibility**: All processing implemented in R with documented scenarios and sensitivity checks, enabling validation and adaptation by other regions.

**Strategic categorisation framework**: The three-category typology (co-located, specialised niches, diversification) translates analytical outputs into actionable investment strategies.

##### Methodological Rigor and Credibility

Several features underscore the analysis's robustness:

- **Official data sources**: Exclusive reliance on publicly available, nationwide statistical datasets (ONS, UKRI, OECD, Companies House) ensures transparency and replicability
- **Multi-scenario testing**: Eight allocation scenarios tested to assess sensitivity of findings to methodological choices
- **Manual validation**: Three rounds of review refining keyword classification through examination of top grants and unassigned records
- **Transparent limitations**: Explicit documentation of data constraints and processing assumptions
- **Open methodology**: Keyword dictionary accessible for scrutiny and refinement; R implementation enables reproducibility
- **Comparative benchmarking**: Analysis spans 11 MCAs plus London, grounding South Yorkshire's position in national context

This rigorous foundation ensures the Investment Playbook's findings rest on defensible, reproducible analysis rather than anecdotal evidence or selective data. The framework's systematic approach to integrating production and innovation metrics provides a credible basis for identifying South Yorkshire's distinctive strengths in Advanced Manufacturing, Clean Energy, Defence, and Life Sciences, as well as strategic opportunities at the intersections of these sectors.
