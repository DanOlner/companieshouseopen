What this shows

Every UK company filing accounts at Companies House reports an employee count for the current period and the previous one. Treating those as two timepoints gives each sector in each local authority a location quotient at both, and the movement between them.

Horizontal: location quotient on a log scale. Right of the blue line, the sector takes a bigger share of local employment than it does of GB employment.

Vertical: either the change in that LQ, or the change in the raw employee count. They differ, and the difference matters — a sector can add people while its LQ falls, because it grew faster somewhere else. The scale is pseudo-log, so gridlines are uneven: without it a single sector doubling would flatten everything else onto the zero line.

Click a circle to drill into the SIC codes inside it. The sector you came from stays on the plot as a large faint circle so you can see how its parts sit against the whole.

With two places showing, hovering a sector rings it in both panels at once. If it isn't in the other panel, that panel says why: either the filters have hidden it (with the employee count, so you can see how far off the threshold it is) or the place has none recorded at all.

Only firms reporting an employee count in both years are counted, which is about 71% of them. Companies House records a registered address, not a workplace, so multi-site firms land entirely at their registered office. Treat this as a view of company registrations, not a jobs census.
