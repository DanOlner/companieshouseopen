# Veedol — Company Lineage, Ownership & Business Connections

Research note prepared 2026-06-15. Origin: the Rotherham-registered "Veedol" appears
to have a history far predating its incorporation date, prompting a dig into its
true lineage and ownership.

## Short answer

The Rotherham company is **not** the historic Veedol — it's a UK distribution
subsidiary that adopted the brand name. Veedol is ultimately **Indian-owned**: both
the Rotherham firm and the linked Scottish firm are controlled out of Kolkata by
**Veedol Corporation Limited** (formerly *Tide Water Oil Co. (India) Ltd*), part of
the **Andrew Yule & Company** group. The "history back to 1913" on
veedol.co.uk/history.php is the story of the global *brand*, not of the legal entity
registered in Rotherham (which is a 1998 shelf company that only took the Veedol name
in 2019).

## The two Companies House entities

### Veedol UK Limited — 03564422 (Rotherham)

A UK operating/distribution company, **not** the brand's origin.

- Registered office: Unit 29 Goldthorpe Industrial Estate, Goldthorpe, Rotherham,
  South Yorkshire, S63 9BL. Status: Active. SIC 19201 (mineral oil refining).
- Incorporated **14 May 1998** as a shelf company, **GLS 167 Limited**.
- Renamed **Price Thomas Holdings Limited** (19 Jun 1998) — run by **Michael Price
  Thomas** (director 1998–2016) and Susanne Price-Thomas (secretary 2000–2016). This
  was the Price-Thomas family's UK Veedol distribution business.
- The Indian group took control on **28 April 2016** (PSC notified 6 April 2016;
  Indian directors appointed, Price-Thomases resigned).
- Renamed **Veedol UK Limited** on **3 Dec 2019**.
- **PSC:** owned 75%+ (shares, voting rights, right to appoint/remove directors) by
  **Veedol Corporation Limited**, India — registered office *8 Yule House, Dr Rajendra
  Prasad Sarani, Kolkata 700001*.
- Current directors all India-resident: Praveen Kadle (2020–), Upendra Vinayak Gadre
  (2025–), Rajendra Nath Ghosal (2026–).

#### What "shelf company" means here

A **shelf company** is one that's been incorporated and then left dormant so it can be
**sold to a buyer later** — it sits "on the shelf" like ready-made stock, nothing to do
with selling on products. The pattern is visible in this firm's filing history: the
generic batch name **GLS 167 Limited**, **nominee** officers (Group Captain John William
King, Justine Falkner, and GLS Financial Services Limited as secretary) who resigned
within months, and a rename to **Price Thomas Holdings Limited** just five weeks after
incorporation when the real owner took over. Buying a shelf company was historically a
quick way to get a company without waiting for incorporation, and an aged one can look
more established to banks or tender processes.

Using a shelf company is a completely **legitimate and common** business practice — but
because it provides a ready-made entity with a pre-existing incorporation date, the
mechanism is also occasionally exploited to disguise a business as older or more
established than it really is. In Veedol UK's case it is entirely mundane: a family firm
bought a shelf company in 1998 to set up their UK Veedol distribution business, and that
same legal shell was later renamed again (2019) when the Indian parent took it over.

### Veedol International Limited — SC054624 (Edinburgh, originally Glasgow)

The **global trademark-holding company** — directly linked to the Rotherham firm.

- Registered office: 4th Floor, 115 George Street, Edinburgh, EH2 4JN. Status: Active.
- Incorporated **28 November 1973**; historic French/Turkish/Irish directors reflect
  its earlier life as the European Veedol entity.
- Holds worldwide rights to the Veedol trademarks, logos and product sub-brands.
- Was owned by **BP** and **acquired 100% by Tide Water Oil Co. (India) in October
  2011**.
- Now run by the same India-resident directors (Chandavarkar, Gadre, Ghosal) — same
  control as the Rotherham firm. PSC filing states "no registrable person" (held via
  the Indian parent structure rather than an individual).

## Why the history predates incorporation — the global brand lineage

1. **1913** — Veedol created by Platt & Washburn Refining Co., a subsidiary of the
   **Tide Water Oil Company** (Pennsylvania, USA). Became the engine oil used in Henry
   Ford's Model T; later used on the *Graf Zeppelin*, the 1931 *Miss Veedol* first
   non-stop trans-Pacific flight, and (synthetics) Space Shuttle *Columbia* (1979).
2. **1926 → 1956** — Tide Water Associated Oil → renamed **Tidewater Oil Company**
   (Flying A + Veedol brands).
3. **1960s** — Phillips Petroleum bought the western operations; **Getty Oil** took the
   eastern ones. Getty was later absorbed by **Texaco** (1984).
4. The international Veedol rights ended up with **Burmah-Castrol**; **BP acquired
   Burmah-Castrol in 2000**, and with it Veedol and the Scottish trademark company.
5. **October 2011** — BP sold **Veedol International Limited** and the global trademark
   portfolio to **Tide Water Oil Co. (India) Ltd**.

## Who owns it now

- **Tide Water Oil Co. (India) Ltd** — making lubricants in India since 1928 (itself
  originally a US Tide Water subsidiary), part of the **Andrew Yule & Company** group, a
  Government of India public-sector enterprise headquartered in Kolkata. Publicly listed
  in India (revenue ~₹1,990 Cr FY2025).
- It **renamed itself "Veedol Corporation Limited" in September 2024** — exactly the
  entity named as the controlling PSC of the Rotherham company.

So the Rotherham registration is just where the UK arm sits; ultimate ownership and the
brand's home are now Indian (Kolkata), having passed:

> **US (Tidewater) → Getty / Texaco → Burmah-Castrol / BP → Tide Water Oil (India)**

## Reliability note

The BP/Burmah-Castrol → Tide Water India chain and the 2011 acquisition are well
attested. The precise pre-2000 European trademark path (how Veedol's international rights
reached Burmah-Castrol) is the fuzziest link if it needs nailing down further.
